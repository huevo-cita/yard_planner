# Austin / Travis County Planting Calendar — Zone 9a

For a raised vegetable bed in Central Texas. Replace this file for any other location.

## Frost dates: use a range, not a number

Sources disagree by weeks, and the spread is the useful information.

| Source | Last spring frost | First fall frost |
|---|---|---|
| Camp Mabry (Austin's central station) | ~Feb 20 | ~Dec 1 |
| Austin Great Hills | ~Feb 25 | ~Nov 27 |
| Austin-Bergstrom (ABIA) | ~Mar 5 | ~Nov 13 |
| Travis County 31-yr median | Mar 6 | Nov 23 |
| Conservative / safe | Apr 5 | Dec 11 |
| Aggressive / risky | Feb 10 | Nov 1 |

In 8 years out of 10 the last frost lands somewhere between Feb 10 and Apr 5 — a 54-day window.

**How to use it.** Plan transplanting to the aggressive end to get ahead of the June heat, and
keep frost cloth on hand rather than waiting for safety. In Central Texas, a tomato planted in
mid-March and protected twice beats a tomato planted in April that never sizes up before the
heat shuts it down. Losing a flat of transplants to one late freeze is the cheaper mistake.

## The four-phase year

Austin doesn't run the two-season calendar most gardening advice assumes. It has four phases,
and the summer one is a genuine dead zone.

### Spring warm season — transplant late Feb through mid Mar

Short and front-loaded. Fruit set on tomatoes stops once nights stay above ~75°F, which is
typically early-to-mid June, so anything transplanted after mid-March runs out of runway.
Choose varieties in the 60–75 day range; long-season heirlooms rarely finish here.

| Crop | Start indoors | Transplant / sow | Harvest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tomato | Jan 10 – Jan 25 | Transplant Feb 20 – Mar 15 | May – late June |
| Pepper (sweet + hot) | Jan 10 – Jan 25 | Transplant Mar 1 – Apr 10 | May – July, often again in fall |
| Eggplant | Jan 10 – Jan 25 | Transplant Mar 10 – May | May – July |
| Basil | Feb | Transplant/sow Mar 15 – Apr | May – Oct |
| Cucumber | — | Direct sow Mar 1 – Apr 1 | May – June |
| Bush/pole beans | — | Direct sow Mar 10 – Apr 15 | May – June |
| Melon | Feb (optional) | Sow Mar 15 – Apr 30 | June – July |
| Squash / zucchini | — | Direct sow Mar 1 – Apr 1 | May – June |
| Corn | — | Direct sow Mar | June |

Peppers and eggplant want warmer soil than tomatoes — hold them 1–3 weeks later. Soil
temperature matters more than the calendar: 60°F at 4 inches for tomatoes, 65°F for peppers,
eggplant, and everything in the summer list. A $10 soil thermometer removes the guesswork.

### Summer survival — sow May through mid July

June through August, soil at 4 inches runs 75–85°F and air hits 100°F. Lettuce, brassicas and
most fruiting crops are done. Four things genuinely thrive.

| Crop | Min soil temp | Sow window | Days to harvest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Okra | 65°F (75°F optimal) | May 1 – Jul 15 | 50–65 |
| Southern peas (cowpeas, purple hull, crowder, zipper cream) | 65°F | May 1 – Jul 15 | 60–80 |
| Sweet potato (slips) | 65°F | Apr 15 – Jun 30 | 70–120 |
| Malabar spinach | warm | May – Jun | 50–70, cut-and-come-again |

Hot peppers and eggplant also hold through summer once established, though they slow down.

Southern peas do double duty: they're an edible crop and a nitrogen-fixing cover crop, which
makes them the right answer for a bed you'd otherwise rest through the summer gap.

Summer management: mulch heavily, water deep in the early morning, and consider 30–40% shade
cloth over anything struggling. Surface temperatures above 95°F stall germination, so
newly-sown seed needs shade until it's up.

### Fall warm season — transplant mid July through mid Aug

Austin's second tomato season, and often the better one — fewer pests, and fruit ripens into
cooling nights.

Sources put the window anywhere from July 1 to Sept 1, and the tradeoff is real. Earlier
planting gives more time to mature before frost but means establishing a transplant in brutal
heat. Later is easier on the plant but risks frost before the fruit ripens. **Mid-July to
early August is the defensible middle**, with shade cloth over new transplants for two weeks.

Also plantable: bush beans (Aug), cucumbers (Aug), summer squash (Aug).

### Cool season — plant Sept through Oct

The longest and most reliable phase. Holds the bed from September into February.

| Crop | Method | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Carrots | Direct sow | Early Sept – Oct |
| Broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower | Transplant | Sept – mid Oct |
| Kale, collards, chard | Transplant | Sept – Oct |
| Lettuce | Sow + transplant, succession every 2 weeks | Sept – Feb |
| Spinach | Direct sow | Oct – Nov |
| Radish | Direct sow, succession every 2–3 weeks | Sept – Feb |
| Beets, turnips | Direct sow | Sept – Oct |
| Garlic | Cloves | Mid Oct (harvests May) |
| Cilantro, dill, parsley | Sow or transplant | Sept – Oct, thrives all winter |
| Onions | Transplants/sets | Jan – Feb |
| English/snap peas | Direct sow | Jan – Feb |

Cool-season crops here mostly shrug off frost, and kale and carrots taste better after one.
Garlic occupies its square from October to May, so plan around it across two phases.

## Seed-starting lead times

Count back from the target transplant date:

| Crop | Weeks before transplant |
|---|---|
| Tomato, pepper, eggplant | 6–8 |
| Broccoli, cabbage, kale | 5–6 |
| Cucumber, squash, melon | 3–4 |
| Lettuce | 4 |

Direct-sown crops — carrots, radish, beans, peas, okra, southern peas, spinach — resent
transplanting. Sow them in place.

## The one thing that surprises people

There is no single "growing season" here. There are two productive windows separated by a
summer that kills most things, and the fall window is often better than spring. Planning as
though Austin has a Northern-style May-to-September season produces a bed that's empty when it
should be full and struggling when it should be resting.
